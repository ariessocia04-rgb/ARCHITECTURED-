# VOLUME 1 — SCOPE, GOVERNANCE, OPERATING MODES, AND FULL DEPARTMENT TEAMS

## 1. Business scope

Supports lawful retail of groceries, household goods, general merchandise, apparel, electronics, pharmacy-adjacent non-prescription goods where legally permitted, fresh/frozen goods, weighed goods, bundles, serial/lot-controlled goods, consignment items, and services.

## 2. Operating modes

### Solo Seller / Owner-Cashier

One person can sell, receive stock, monitor cash, record expenses, check profit, manage suppliers, and view simple reports. Advanced modules remain hidden.

### Sari-Sari / Micro Store

- fast sale using search, favorites, barcode, or manual item buttons
- tingi / partial-unit selling
- customer credit ledger with limits and reminders
- simple stock count and low-stock alerts
- cash-in/cash-out and daily close
- supplier purchase reminders

### Small Store

Adds employees, shifts, purchase orders, receiving, pricing, promotions, returns, customer accounts, and basic accounting exports.

### Medium Grocery

Adds departments, multiple registers, warehouse/backroom, fresh/expiry management, weighing scales, batch/lot control, replenishment, receiving teams, finance, HR, and CRM.

### Large Department Store

Adds floors, sections, concessions, category management, advanced procurement, central warehouse, loss prevention, multi-branch, ecommerce/omnichannel, centralized finance/accounting, HR, AI, and enterprise analytics.

## 4. Role compression

Small businesses may assign several roles to one person. The architecture preserves action attribution, permissions, and approval context. Growth delegates existing capabilities to new staff instead of requiring a new system.
